## About the project

### Inspiration

We run Euphoria, an app that teaches teenagers to invest. The thing we hear most is that investing feels scary: the words, the charts, the fear of losing money. For Kiroween we took that literally. What if the fear was a haunted house, and every ghost in it was someone who got money wrong? You do not fight the ghosts. You learn what they never understood, and that sets them free.

### What it does

Euphoria Haunted House is a 3D pixel-art game that runs in the browser, on desktop and on a phone.

- You walk a dark room with a lantern. Moonlight comes through arched windows, jack-o'-lanterns flicker, and a ghost blocks the door.
- Three glowing wisps mark things the ghost left behind: a drawer, two portraits, a grandfather clock. Looking at one plays a short fullscreen pixel movie of the lesson, then lights one of the room's three candles.
- With all three candles lit, the ghost faces you. It asks real questions from the lesson. Each right answer drains its unrest. A wrong answer makes the room flicker, the ghost explains the mistake, and the question comes back.
- When the ghost is freed, warm light fills the room, the door unseals, and you walk into the next room.

Three rooms are playable today. The Foyer teaches what investing is, with the Miser, who hid every dollar in a drawer and watched it buy less each year. The Cellar teaches risk and reward, with the Gambler, who bet everything on one stock. The Clock Tower teaches compound interest, with the Clockmaker, who always meant to start tomorrow. Two more rooms are planned: stocks versus bonds, and diversification.

The spooky design is not decoration. Light is the progress bar. The ghost's mood is the feedback. The flicker on a wrong answer is the moment the lesson lands.

### How we built it

- **A 3D scene drawn as pixel art.** We render a three.js scene at a low resolution with toon shading, then a composite pass adds outlines, moonlit rim light and a violet and warm colour grade, and snaps every pixel to one Halloween palette. The camera is orthographic, tilted to a 3-4-5 angle and snapped to the pixel grid, so every texel lands on exactly one screen pixel.
- **2D sprites in a 3D world.** The player and the ghosts are pixel sprites that always face the camera. All of the art is plain text: rows of characters plus a palette, generated by scripts and passed through a pixel-art fixer that checks the grid, removes stray pixels and adds a moonlit edge.
- **Euphoria's own lessons, in costume.** The movies are Euphoria's story lessons, played on Euphoria's story engine and dressed for Halloween: summer greens become autumn rust, and each scene gets bats, jack-o'-lanterns, gravestones and fog. Every question, option and explanation is read straight from the lesson content, so the teaching is exactly what Euphoria teaches.
- **Sound with no files.** The drone, wind, footsteps and the music-box score are all synthesised live with the Web Audio API.
- **Rooms as data.** A room is a 3D build plus a script, so adding a lesson means adding one room.

### Challenges we ran into

- **Light in three.js is divided by pi.** Our first room rendered almost black. Ambient and moon light needed values about three times larger than they looked like they should.
- **A hanging chandelier looked like junk on the rug.** With a top-down camera, anything hanging in the middle of a room lands on the floor on screen. We replaced it with a warm light that swells through the room when the ghost is freed.
- **Moonlight spilled over the back wall** and drew a hard bright band across the floor. Lowering the moon made the wall's shadow cover the floor, and made the window beams longer and more dramatic.
- **Bright gold snapped to mint green.** The palette snap picked the nearest colour, and for over-lit gold that was a green. We removed the greens from the 3D palette, because nothing in the rooms is green.
- **Reskinning without redrawing.** Euphoria's story art uses its own colours, so a simple palette swap matched nothing. We shift every green toward autumn by hue instead, which reskins any scene without touching its drawing.

### Accomplishments that we're proud of

- A 3D room that reads as hand-made pixel art, with real moonbeams through real window openings.
- Lessons that play as short movies, then come back as a ghost's questions.
- A complete loop that works on a phone, with keyboard and touch controls and a reduced-motion mode.
- Being honest about reuse: the README lists exactly what came from Euphoria and from our earlier work.

### What we learned

Fear is a good teacher when it has a shape. A ghost who lost his savings to inflation is easier to remember than a paragraph about inflation. We also learned that atmosphere has to carry information to earn its place: the darkness works because lighting a candle means you learned something.

### What's next for Euphoria Haunted House
